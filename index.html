About

I’m an electrical engineer with over 15 years of experience in marine and offshore power systems. I spent most of my career at sea with Rigel, Maersk and Royal Caribbean, where as 1st Electrical Engineer I led a team of seven and managed major upgrade projects, from propulsion and automation retrofits to a full radio-navigation replacement.

Today I work onshore as a Senior Design Engineer on offshore wind substation projects, focusing on wiring diagrams, FAT/SAT test documentation and O&M manuals. I enjoy work where technology, people and clear communication meet.

Outside work, I’m a Linux enthusiast, especially Debian, and a supporter of the free software ideas promoted by the Free Software Foundation.

Usefull Links

  • Free Software Foundation (FSF) - A non-profit organization founded in 1985 by Richard Stallman. It promotes software freedom, meaning users’ right to run, study, change and share programs. The FSF supports the GNU Project and maintains the GNU General Public License (GPL), one of the most widely used free software licenses.

  • git - A free, open-source version control system. It tracks every change in a project, so you can go back to earlier versions, work on several ideas at once in separate branches, and work with others on the same code. Linus Torvalds created it in 2005 for Linux, and today it is the standard tool for programmers everywhere.

  • wiby - A search engine for the “classic web.” It finds simple, lightweight pages made by people about their interests, like the early internet before ads and heavy websites. There is also a “surprise me” button that opens a random page.

  • marginalia-search.com - An independent search engine with custom-built code that deliberately favors text-heavy websites, personal blogs, and niche forums while filtering out modern, "heavy" corporate portals. It is designed to help you discover a web dominated by content, not modern design.

  • debian.org - rock-solid, community-driven Linux operating system renowned for its exceptional stability, robust security, and strict commitment to free, open-source software.
